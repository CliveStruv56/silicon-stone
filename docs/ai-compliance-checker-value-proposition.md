# AI Act Compliance Checker

## A practical first-pass assessment tool for SMEs using AI

The AI Act Compliance Checker helps organisations understand how an AI tool or AI-enabled product may sit under the EU AI Act. It is designed for SMEs that use third-party AI systems, evaluate AI vendors, or build AI-enabled products for market.

The tool does not replace legal advice. Its purpose is to create a defensible first-pass AI system record: a structured assessment of the tool, the likely risk route, the evidence needed from vendors, and the governance actions the organisation should take next.

## The Problem It Solves

Most SMEs are already using AI through third-party platforms, productivity suites, customer support systems, HR tools, marketing software, analytics products, and sector-specific applications. In many cases, AI functionality has been added by vendors without a clear internal review process.

This creates several practical problems:

- The organisation may not know which AI systems it uses.
- It may not know whether it is a deployer, provider, or both.
- It may not know whether a tool is minimal-risk, transparency-risk, high-risk, GPAI-related, uncertain, or potentially prohibited.
- It may not know what documentation to ask vendors for.
- It may not know when an AI tool should be reassessed after launch.
- It may confuse AI Act duties with adjacent GDPR, data protection, and vendor contract risks.

The checker is built to turn that uncertainty into a structured record and a clear next-action list.

## What The Tool Does

The checker guides the user through a structured assessment covering:

- why the tool is being assessed
- whether the organisation is using, integrating, modifying, reselling, or building the system
- EU scope and market connection
- the system's primary use case
- who is affected by the system's outputs
- whether the system influences or determines decisions about people
- human oversight and override arrangements
- sensitive AI Act domains such as employment, education, healthcare, credit, biometrics, public services, critical infrastructure, law enforcement, migration, justice, and democratic processes
- prohibited-practice red flags
- personal, employee, health, biometric, child, or special-category data use
- transparency triggers such as chatbots, AI-generated content, synthetic media, and biometric or emotion-related disclosure duties
- vendor evidence already held by the organisation
- events that should trigger future reassessment

The result is not just a label. It produces a practical system record.

## Outputs Provided

Each completed assessment produces:

- likely AI Act classification
- confidence level
- likely organisational role: deployer, provider, both, or unclear
- explanation of why the result was reached
- missing evidence that could change the result
- immediate obligations or governance actions
- vendor due diligence questions
- adjacent GDPR and vendor-risk signals
- ongoing reassessment triggers
- official source references
- versioned rules fired by the assessment engine

This makes the tool useful before, during, and after AI Act implementation dates. It is not a one-off deadline checker. It is an ongoing AI governance and vendor-risk workflow.

## What Makes It Different From A Generic Lead Magnet

Many compliance tools give a simple risk label after a short quiz. This checker is designed to provide usable operational value.

### 1. Rule-Based Assessment

The tool uses a structured rule library rather than a generic chatbot response. Each conclusion is tied to a rule with:

- rule ID
- rule version
- source article or legal basis
- legal status
- evidence from the user's answers
- explanation of the rule's relevance
- missing facts or vendor evidence required

This means a user can see why a result was produced and what facts drove it.

### 2. Article-Level Evidence Trail

The assessment references official AI Act structures, including:

- territorial scope
- prohibited practices
- high-risk classification
- Annex III high-risk areas
- deployer obligations
- transparency obligations
- GPAI and systemic-risk considerations

The paid report can expand these into a full evidence pack showing which rules fired, what answer triggered each one, and what the organisation should do next.

### 3. Built For SMEs Using Third-Party AI

Most SMEs are not building foundation models. They are using vendor tools. The checker reflects that reality.

It asks whether the organisation is:

- using a third-party system as supplied
- configuring or integrating a vendor tool
- materially modifying, fine-tuning, rebranding, or reselling it
- building its own AI product
- unsure of its role

This distinction is commercially important because many SMEs need deployer guidance and vendor due diligence, not abstract AI policy commentary.

### 4. Vendor Due Diligence Built In

The tool identifies what evidence the user should request from the vendor, including:

- AI Act classification
- intended-purpose statement
- instructions for use
- human oversight guidance
- limitations and misuse warnings
- data processing agreement
- retention and sub-processor terms
- audit logs and export options
- model update or feature-change notification policy

This is immediate practical value for procurement, compliance, operations, and management teams.

### 5. Ongoing Governance, Not Deadline Panic

The checker is designed to remain useful after implementation dates pass. It asks what should trigger reassessment, such as:

- new use case
- new department
- new data category
- increased automation
- reduced human review
- vendor model or terms change
- complaint from a customer, worker, or regulator
- annual AI register review

The output can therefore support a living AI system register.

## Example Use Cases

### Vendor Procurement

An SME is considering an AI-powered HR screening tool. The checker identifies the likely high-risk employment route, asks about candidate ranking and human oversight, and produces vendor questions before procurement proceeds.

### Existing AI Inventory

A business wants to understand where AI is already being used. Each tool can be assessed and turned into a system record with ownership, purpose, risk signals, vendor evidence gaps, and review triggers.

### Customer Chatbot Review

A company deploys an AI customer support assistant. The checker identifies transparency obligations, prompts the user to check disclosure wording, and asks whether the chatbot influences significant decisions.

### Product Vendor Assessment

A SaaS company adds an AI feature to its own product. The checker identifies possible provider-side responsibilities and distinguishes ordinary deployer use from building, modifying, or selling an AI system.

### Board Or Audit Request

The checker creates a structured summary that can be used to brief management: what the system does, who is affected, what risk route applies, what evidence is missing, and when reassessment is required.

## Why This Matters

AI compliance is not a one-time deadline exercise. AI systems change, vendor models change, use cases expand, and levels of automation increase. A tool that only counts down to an implementation date quickly becomes obsolete.

The AI Act Compliance Checker is designed as an ongoing reference and assessment workflow. It helps organisations ask the right questions whenever they adopt, renew, modify, or review an AI system.

The AI Act Compliance Checker gives SMEs a structured way to move from uncertainty to action.

3. What evidence is missing?
