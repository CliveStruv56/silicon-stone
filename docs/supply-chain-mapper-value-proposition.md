# Supply Chain Mapper

## A practical semiconductor exposure diagnostic for industrial teams

The Supply Chain Mapper helps organisations understand where their semiconductor and technology supply chains may be exposed to concentration risk, geopolitical disruption, and sub-tier dependency.

It is designed for SMEs, industrial operators, procurement teams, technology leaders, and regional economic decision-makers who depend on chips but do not have the resources of a dedicated semiconductor intelligence function.

The tool does not claim to replace a full supplier audit or enterprise supply-chain risk platform. Its purpose is to create a defensible first-pass exposure record: a structured view of the likely chokepoints, the scenarios that matter, the supplier questions to ask, and the mitigation moves the organisation should consider next.

## The Problem It Solves

Most businesses do not buy semiconductors directly from foundries. They buy modules, boards, industrial systems, cloud capacity, vehicles, AI hardware, medical devices, networking equipment, and embedded electronics.

That means semiconductor risk is often hidden several layers below the visible supplier relationship.

This creates several practical problems:

- The organisation may not know which chip classes its operations depend on.
- It may not know whether a disruption would come from wafer fabrication, advanced packaging, memory, materials, equipment, EDA software, or export controls.
- It may assume a finished-goods supplier has diversified supply when the underlying chip dependency is still single-source.
- It may not know which supplier questions to ask before a shortage or geopolitical shock.
- It may confuse ordinary procurement risk with structural semiconductor chokepoints.
- It may lack a board-ready way to explain why a technical supply-chain issue matters commercially.

The mapper is built to turn that uncertainty into a structured exposure view and a clear next-action list.

## What Makes It Different From A Generic Lead Magnet

Many supply-chain tools show a world map or a generic risk score. This mapper is designed to provide usable operational value in a specific domain: semiconductor and advanced technology dependency.

### 1. Chokepoint-Based Semiconductor Model

The tool is structured around real semiconductor supply-chain layers rather than generic supplier categories.

It distinguishes between:

- fabs
- materials
- equipment
- design
- advanced packaging
- IP and EDA

This matters because a user facing an AI hardware shortage, automotive electronics delay, or industrial control component issue needs to know which layer is constraining supply.

### 2. Scenario-Based Exposure

The mapper includes stress scenarios that change which nodes matter most.

Current scenarios include:

- baseline conditions
- Taiwan disruption
- EUV equipment constraint
- HBM shortage
- export-control escalation

This helps users move from static risk awareness to practical contingency thinking.

### 3. Organisation-Specific Diagnostic

The tool asks for operating context rather than showing every user the same answer.

It adjusts exposure based on:

- chip class
- sourcing flexibility
- operating footprint
- selected disruption scenario

This makes the output more useful for an industrial manufacturer than a generic semiconductor explainer would be.

### 4. Supplier Questions Built In

The mapper identifies specific questions the user should take back to suppliers, vendors, procurement teams, or internal technical owners.

Examples include:

- Which products depend on advanced-node TSMC capacity?
- Which package technology does the accelerator use?
- Are second sources qualified or only theoretically available?
- What allocation rights exist during constraint?
- Could export controls affect toolchain, component, or material access?

This is immediate practical value because many organisations do not know how to interrogate sub-tier semiconductor exposure.

### 5. Evidence And Confidence Markers

The tool is designed to support an evidence trail.

Each richer node can include:

- source references
- last reviewed date
- confidence level
- market position note
- risk profile
- mitigation options

The paid report can expand this into a more formal evidence pack showing what was assessed, what assumptions were made, and where deeper investigation is needed.

## Example Use Cases

### AI Hardware Procurement

A company plans to buy GPU capacity or AI servers. The mapper identifies exposure to TSMC advanced logic, CoWoS-like packaging, HBM supply, substrate constraints, and export-control risk. It gives procurement specific allocation and substitution questions before the purchase is committed.

### Automotive Electronics Exposure

An industrial or automotive supplier wants to understand whether its exposure is primarily mature-node chips, materials, power electronics, or China-linked critical minerals. The mapper helps frame supplier conversations around actual semiconductor layers rather than generic component shortages.

### Board Risk Briefing

An operations director needs to explain why a Taiwan event, EUV delay, or HBM shortage could affect delivery schedules. The printable snapshot provides a concise top-exposure list, first response moves, and supplier questions.

### Supplier Review

A procurement team is renewing a critical vendor contract. The mapper helps them ask about foundry dependency, qualified alternates, package technology, memory allocation, and export-control exposure.

### Scenario Planning

A leadership team wants to stress-test assumptions. The mapper allows them to compare baseline conditions with Taiwan disruption, EUV constraint, HBM shortage, and export-control escalation.

## Why This Matters

Semiconductor supply-chain risk is not a one-time shortage story. It is a structural feature of the digital economy.

Advanced chips depend on tightly concentrated layers: EUV lithography, high-purity materials, advanced packaging, HBM, substrates, EDA software, and regionally concentrated manufacturing capacity. A business may never buy a wafer directly, but it can still be exposed to these constraints through hardware, cloud, automation systems, vehicles, industrial controls, or customer products.

A tool that only shows a map is useful for awareness. A tool that connects the map to the user's operating context, supplier questions, scenarios, and mitigation moves becomes commercially useful.
