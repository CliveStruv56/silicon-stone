# Legal Firm Partnership Brief

**Purpose:** Position Silicon & Stone as the technical, operational, and industry-context partner for legal firms advising mid-market clients on AI Act readiness, AI governance, vendor evidence, and technology dependency.

**Core thesis:** Legal firms can interpret the law and advise on legal obligations. Many of their clients still struggle to describe what AI systems they use, where those systems touch customers, employees, vendors, and data, and what evidence exists behind the use of those systems. Silicon & Stone fills that gap.

---

## 1. The Approach

Do not approach legal firms with a broad request to "partner". Approach them with a specific client-facing product they can sell or pilot.

The recommended first offer is:

## AI Exposure & Legal Readiness Diagnostic

A fixed-scope diagnostic for mid-market organisations that need to understand their AI exposure before legal advice can become concrete.

The legal firm owns the legal advice. Silicon & Stone owns the technical and operational mapping layer.

The strongest opening message:

> Many AI Act conversations stall because the client cannot describe what systems they use, what role they play, what vendor evidence exists, or where AI touches customers, employees, procurement, and data. Silicon & Stone provides the technical and operational mapping layer that makes your legal advice faster, more concrete, and easier for clients to act on.

The initial ask should be low-risk:

- A 30-minute partner conversation.
- A co-hosted private client briefing.
- One pilot diagnostic with an existing client.
- A clear division of responsibilities from the start.

Avoid leading with a revenue-share discussion. Start with usefulness, then define commercial terms once the firm sees the client demand.

## 2. Target Legal Firm Size

The best initial targets are **mid-sized and regional commercial law firms**, not top-tier international firms.

### Ideal firm profile

- 30 to 250 lawyers.
- Commercial, technology, data protection, IP, employment, or regulatory practice.
- Strong SME and mid-market client base.
- Clients in technology, manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, recruitment, education, professional services, or B2B SaaS.
- Interested in AI but unlikely to have deep in-house technical delivery capacity.
- Trusted by clients but not already selling a mature AI governance platform or consulting product.

### Why this size works

Large firms may already have AI governance teams, consulting arms, or global partnerships. Very small firms may lack the client base or appetite for a structured AI-readiness offer.

Mid-sized firms are more likely to have clients asking AI questions without a complete internal answer. They need practical support that helps them advise clients without pretending to be technologists.

### Best practice areas to approach

- Data protection and privacy.
- Technology and commercial contracts.
- IP and technology licensing.
- Employment law, especially workplace AI and automated decision-making.
- Regulatory and compliance.
- Corporate/commercial teams serving scale-ups and mid-market firms.

The best individual sponsor is probably a partner who already advises on data protection, technology contracts, procurement, or regulatory compliance.

## 4. The Division of Responsibility

The boundary must be explicit. It protects both sides and makes the partnership easier to approve.

### Silicon & Stone does

- Map the client's actual AI usage.
- Interview operational, technical, procurement, HR, and leadership stakeholders.
- Translate technical systems into plain-language risk/exposure descriptions.
- Review vendor materials for evidence gaps.
- Identify operational dependencies and supply-chain issues.
- Explain industry context and practical AI adoption patterns.
- Coach leadership teams on what the findings mean operationally.
- Produce non-legal findings and recommendations.

### Silicon & Stone does not

- Provide legal advice.
- Interpret legislation as formal counsel.
- Sign off legal compliance.
- Draft legal opinions.
- Replace privacy, employment, commercial, or regulatory advice.
- Claim that a client is compliant or non-compliant as a legal conclusion.

### The legal firm does

- Interpret the law.
- Advise on the client's legal role and obligations.
- Draft or review legal documents, policies, contracts, and notices.
- Advise on enforcement, liability, contractual exposure, and regulatory risk.
- Decide what needs to be privileged.
- Own legal sign-off.

### Shared work

- Client intake.
- Scope definition.
- Executive briefing.
- Prioritised action plan.
- Follow-on support proposal.

The language to use:

> Silicon & Stone provides technical exposure mapping and operational interpretation. The legal firm provides legal advice. The combined offer gives clients a clearer route from "we use AI somewhere" to "we know what needs legal, operational, procurement, and governance action."

## 5. Partnership Models

Start with the simplest model and evolve only if demand appears.

### Model A: Subcontractor to the legal firm

The legal firm sells the diagnostic to the client and invoices the client. Silicon & Stone invoices the law firm.

**Best for:** First pilots, firms that want to control the client relationship, and matters where legal privilege or client handling is sensitive.

**Pros:** Clean for the client, low-friction for the firm, clear responsibility.

**Cons:** Silicon & Stone may be less visible to the client unless agreed upfront.

### Model B: Co-branded diagnostic

Both parties are visible. The legal firm provides legal interpretation; Silicon & Stone provides technical mapping and operational advisory.

**Best for:** Webinars, published offers, repeatable diagnostics.

**Pros:** Stronger market positioning and clearer combined value.

**Cons:** Requires more agreement on branding, liability, process, and commercial terms.

### Model C: Referral relationship

The legal firm refers clients to Silicon & Stone for technical exposure work, and Silicon & Stone refers legal questions back to the firm.

**Best for:** Informal early-stage relationships.

**Pros:** Simple.

**Cons:** Weakest commercial control; referral-fee rules and professional obligations may complicate payment arrangements.

### Recommended starting model

Start with **Model A for the first paid diagnostic** and **Model B for a webinar/client briefing**.

This lets the firm test demand without building a new practice area and lets Silicon & Stone prove value without asking for a strategic partnership too early.

## 6. The Client Problem To Name

The target client is not waking up thinking "I need Forensic Technopolitics". They are thinking:

- We use AI tools, but nobody has mapped them.
- Our teams are using vendor AI features without central review.
- We need an AI policy but do not know what it should be based on.
- We sell into Europe and are unsure whether the AI Act affects us.
- Procurement is asking vendors weak AI questions.
- The board wants a confident answer, but the facts are scattered.
- Legal counsel needs a factual system map before giving useful advice.

That is the buying trigger.

The strongest client-facing promise:

> In two to three weeks, we will map where AI already touches your organisation, identify the evidence gaps, separate operational actions from legal questions, and give your leadership team a practical route to AI readiness.

## 9. Discovery Questions For The Firm

Use the first conversation to test whether they have real client demand.

- Are clients already asking about AI Act readiness or AI governance?
- Which practice area receives those questions?
- Are the questions coming from legal, compliance, HR, procurement, IT, or the board?
- Do clients know what AI systems they use?
- Does the firm already have a technical partner for AI system mapping?
- Would the firm prefer to sell this as its own diagnostic or as a co-branded offer?
- Are there sectors in the client base where AI exposure is already urgent?
- What would make this easy for partners to introduce to clients?
- What boundary language would the firm need to protect legal advice and professional obligations?

The goal is not to pitch for 30 minutes. The goal is to discover whether the firm has client pull.

## 11. Risks And How To Handle Them

### Risk: The firm sees Silicon & Stone as competition

Position as a technical mapping partner, not legal adviser. Say explicitly that legal conclusions stay with the firm.

### Risk: The firm wants free expertise

Offer one free partner conversation and one co-hosted briefing. Do not do unpaid client diagnostics.

### Risk: The client expects legal certainty from Silicon & Stone

Use boundary language in proposals, reports, and calls. Route legal questions back to the firm.

### Risk: The offer becomes too broad

Keep the first product narrow: AI system inventory, vendor evidence, workflow exposure, leadership summary.

### Risk: The law firm moves slowly

Target several firms at once. The goal is to find one partner with live client demand, not to persuade a slow committee.
