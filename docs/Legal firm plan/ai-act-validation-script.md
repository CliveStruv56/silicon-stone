# The 20-Question Validation Script
### AI Act exposure conversations — mid-cap Europe

> **Purpose:** De-risk the Drift Retainer / Focused Diagnostic offer *before* writing more
> code or launching. Run 20–30 of these with real target buyers. Goal outcome: **3 paid
> diagnostics or 1 signed retainer within 90 days.** Anything less than a paid commitment,
> a named budget, or a warm referral is a polite no.

**Target buyer ("Clara"):** head of compliance / country manager at a 50–600-person European
mid-cap that has deployed AI, has no real governance in place, can't afford a Big Four firm,
has little hands-on AI knowledge, and is worried about sovereignty and upcoming legislation.

---

## The one rule that makes this work

You are not selling. You are not validating your idea — you are trying to **kill** it. The
conversation succeeds when you learn something that surprises you, not when they say "great
idea." The moment you pitch, the data goes worthless: now they're being polite instead of
honest.

Three discipline rules, taped to your monitor:

1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Ask what they've *done*, not what they *think* or *would* do.
2. **Past behaviour > future intentions.** "Have you ever paid for X" beats "Would you pay for X." The first is data; the second is flattery.
3. **Talk less.** Aim for them speaking 80% of the time. Every question below has a "shut up and listen for" — that's the actual point of the question.

**Opening line** (lowers the stakes, gets honesty):

> "I'm not selling anything — I'd genuinely be embarrassed to. I'm building something in the
> AI-governance space and I'm trying to find out whether the problem I think exists actually
> does. You're exactly the person who'd know if I'm wrong. Can I ask you fifteen, twenty
> minutes of honest questions, and you tell me where I'm being naive?"

---

## Section A — Situation & ownership (warm-up, no pain yet)
*Goal: establish the real internal reality. Most "we've got it handled" answers crack here.*

**1.** Walk me through how AI actually gets used in the business day-to-day right now — who's using what, and did anyone sign it off?
> Listen for: shadow AI. "People just started using ChatGPT" = ungoverned exposure they may not have admitted to themselves.

**2.** Who owns "are we compliant with the AI Act" internally — whose name is on it if it goes wrong?
> Listen for: a name, a shrug, or "we haven't assigned that yet." If there's no owner, there's no buyer — note who they say should own it.

**3.** When did the AI Act last come up in a leadership or board conversation, and what triggered it?
> Listen for: a real trigger (a customer asked, an auditor, a near-miss) vs. "it hasn't really." A trigger is a buying signal; silence is a vitamin.

**4.** What have you actually done about it so far — even informally?
> Listen for: nothing / a free PDF / "I read an article." This is your baseline. "Nothing" + worry = your opening. "Nothing" + calm = not a buyer yet.

## Section B — Pain, cost, and consequence
*Goal: separate painkiller from vitamin. Make the consequence concrete and personal.*

**5.** What's the worst realistic version of getting this wrong, for the company — and separately, for *you* personally?
> Listen for: whether the pain is personal. People buy to remove personal risk far faster than corporate risk.

**6.** Has a customer, investor, insurer, or auditor ever asked you to prove how your AI systems are governed? What happened?
> Listen for: the "evidence gap" moment. If a customer's procurement team has asked, urgency is real and dated.

**7.** When you read the AI Act timeline — transparency obligations now, high-risk rules in 2027 — does that feel like a real deadline to you, or one that'll slip?
> Listen for: honesty about whether they think enforcement is real. If they think it'll slip, your urgency leaks — note it, don't argue.

**8.** On a scale of "annoying paperwork" to "could genuinely stop us selling into a market" — where does this sit for you, and why there?
> Listen for: the number AND the reason. The reason is the language you'll put on your website.

## Section C — Past behaviour & spend (the truth serum)
*Goal: find out if money already moves for this kind of problem. This is where "good idea" people get exposed.*

**9.** Who do you currently call when a regulatory or tech-risk question lands that you can't answer in-house?
> Listen for: their existing default — law firm, consultant, Google, nobody. That's your real competitor, named.

**10.** Have you ever paid an outside firm for compliance, legal, or governance help? Who, roughly how much, and were you happy?
> Listen for: a real number. This is your pricing anchor and proof that budget for this category exists at all.

**11.** What did the *decision* to spend that money look like — who signed it off, and what made it an easy or hard yes?
> Listen for: the buying process. Single signature vs. board approval vs. annual budget cycle changes everything about how you sell.

**12.** If you've *not* paid anyone for AI governance specifically — what's stopped you? Budget, time, didn't know who, or didn't feel urgent enough yet?
> Listen for: the actual objection. "Didn't know who to call" is a gift. "Not urgent" is the wall you need to understand.

**13.** Roughly what would a problem like this have to cost the business before someone said "right, we need to spend real money to fix it"?
> Listen for: their internal trigger threshold. Tells you whether your £2,000–£2,500 numbers are trivial or terrifying to them.

## Section D — The alternatives (locate yourself honestly)
*Goal: map yourself against do-nothing, the law firm, and the free PDF.*

**14.** If you decided to sort this properly tomorrow, what are the options you'd weigh up — and who'd be the obvious first call?
> Listen for: whether you even appear in their mental list. If the first call is always "our law firm," that's your partnership thesis confirmed.

**15.** What would make you trust an outside advisor on something this exposed — what do they need to *have* before you'd let them near the board?
> Listen for: credential, logo, indemnity, referral. This tells you exactly what moat you must borrow (your law-firm partnership).

**16.** What's the case *for* just waiting and doing nothing until enforcement actually starts biting?
> Listen for: how strong "do nothing" is for them. It's your biggest competitor — understand its appeal before you try to beat it.

## Section E — The controlled reveal (only now do you describe it)
*Goal: get a genuine reaction to the offer and the price. Watch the face, not the words.*

**17.** Here's roughly what I'm thinking of building — *[describe it in one plain sentence: a senior, independent read on your AI-Act and tech-dependency exposure, monthly, in language your leadership can act on, without a Big-Four price tag]*. What's your honest first reaction — including what's wrong with it?
> Listen for: specific engagement ("would it cover X?") vs. polite warmth ("sounds great!"). Warmth is a no in disguise.

**18.** If that existed and worked the way I described, what would feel like fair money for it — monthly, or for a one-off diagnostic to start?
> Listen for: their number first, before you say yours. Then say your real numbers (£2,500 diagnostic / £2,000-a-month retainer — direct-channel rates as at 2026-08-15; check `/pricing` before the call) and **watch their face for the half-second before they speak.** That flinch, or its absence, is the single most valuable data point in the whole call.

**19.** Is this a "interesting, keep me posted" or a "actually, can you send me something I could act on" — and be brutal, you won't hurt my feelings?
> Listen for: the real temperature. Force the binary. "Keep me posted" = no. "Send me a proposal / when can you start" = the only yes that counts.

## Section F — Close & multiply

**20.** Who else should I be talking to — one or two people in a similar spot who'd give me an equally honest hour?
> Listen for: a warm intro. A referral is the strongest validation of all — people don't spend social capital on ideas they think are duds. No referral offered = quietly a no, however nice the call was.

---

## How to score it afterwards (do this within 10 minutes, while it's fresh)

**A real YES — count these only:**
- Asked you to send a proposal / diagnostic scope, *with* a named budget or timeframe
- Gave you a referral unprompted
- Reacted to the price with negotiation ("could you do it for…") rather than flinch — flinch-then-negotiate is *still* a yes
- Described a specific recent trigger (Q3/Q6) — dated pain, not general worry

**A polite NO dressed as a yes — discard these:**
- "Great idea," "really interesting," "love what you're doing"
- "Send me the newsletter / keep me posted / let's stay in touch"
- Enthusiasm with no money, no name, no date, no referral
- They talked *less* than you did

**The tally that decides it:** Run 20–30 of these. You're looking for **3 paid diagnostics or
1 signed retainer inside 90 days.** Track a one-line outcome per call (template below). By
call 15 the pattern will be obvious — either the pain and the budget are there, or you've
just saved yourself nine months.
