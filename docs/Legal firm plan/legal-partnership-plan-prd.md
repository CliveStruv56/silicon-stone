# PRD — Legal-Firm Partnership Channel

> **One-line goal:** Turn mid-size law firms into a referral and credibility channel for the
> technical/operational side of AI Act readiness — solving the hardest problem in the business
> (how a frightened buyer finds and trusts us) without an 18-month audience build.
>
> **Status:** Proposed · **Owner:** Clive · **Type:** Go-to-market / distribution
> · **Companion docs:** `docs/legal-partner-scope-leaflet.md`, `docs/ai-act-validation-script.md`

---

## 1. Why this, why now

The roast verdict named borrowed credibility and a real distribution channel as the missing
moat. For our buyer — a compliance lead at a mid-cap with no governance and no Big-Four budget
— **the law firm is already in the room.** They don't need to find us; they need their lawyer
to say "for the technical side, talk to these people." That single referral is worth more than
any volume of content, and it arrives pre-trusted.

The structural insight: **a mid-size law firm with AI Act demand and no in-house technical bench
has the same problem its clients have, one level up.** It can advise on the law and then has no
credible answer to "okay, but who actually audits our systems?" We are that answer. We make the
firm's AI practice deliverable.

## 2. The buyer (the firm) and the end-client

- **Partner target:** mid-size / regional-national independent law firms, ~15–80 fee-earners,
  with a named **technology / data-protection / commercial** practice, serving mid-size
  companies (not FTSE 100s), showing public AI-Act activity in the last ~12 months, and lacking
  any in-house technical AI capability.
- **End-client (unchanged):** compliance / country-manager at a 50–600-person European company
  with AI deployed, no governance, no Big-Four budget, low hands-on AI knowledge.

## 3. The non-negotiable: demarcation

The partnership lives or dies on a razor-sharp line between **legal advice (theirs)** and
**operational/technical audit (ours)**. A firm only refers to someone who visibly *cannot* be
mistaken for giving legal advice — so the referral **reduces** their liability rather than
adding to it.

The one-liner, given to every lawyer:
> "You tell them what the law requires. I tell them whether their systems can actually prove
> it — and what to fix if they can't. I never give a legal opinion, and I put that in writing."

The artefact that makes the split real and safe to refer is the **scope-and-boundaries leaflet**
(`docs/legal-partner-scope-leaflet.md`) and the **Evidence Gap Report** as the core deliverable.

## 4. Target selection — sizing rationale

| Band | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Magic Circle / Big-Four legal arms | **Avoid** | Own in-house tech/AI consulting; refer down the hall, not out. No procurement route. |
| Mid-size / regional-national independent (15–80 fee-earners, real tech/DP practice) | **Target** | Have the AI-Act demand *and* the technical credibility gap we fill. Big enough for deal flow, small enough to have no technical bench. |
| High-street / sub-10 general practice | **Avoid** | No AI-Act clients, no deal flow; a referral a quarter is not a channel. |

**Nuance:** some mid-size firms have a GDPR/data-protection specialist who thinks AI Act is
"just more GDPR." That person is champion or blocker. The demarcation pitch (§3) is designed to
disarm them: we take the *technical* work they can't do anyway and make them look more complete.

## 5. Finding the firms — signals & sources

**Buying signals (the firm has already decided AI is a growth area):**
- Published AI Act / AI-governance content — client alerts, webinars, "readiness" guides.
- A hire or promotion into an "AI / emerging tech" role (LinkedIn, firm press).
- An AI / technology / digital-regulation working group; speaking at AI-governance events.
- Ranked under "Data Protection / TMT / Technology" in legal directories.

**Sources for the shortlist:**
- **Legal 500 & Chambers** — mid-tier "Data Protection / TMT / Commercial" rankings, target geographies, *not* Band 1 nationals. Cleanest pre-filtered list.
- **IAPP** member firms and event-sponsor lists.
- **LinkedIn** — title contains "AI"/"technology" + lawyer/solicitor/partner + geography; prioritise those *posting* about the AI Act.
- **Regional/national law-society directories** by practice area.
- **Our own article audience** once Plausible is live — a lawyer reading our AI Act pieces is a warm, self-identified lead.

**Qualification filter (need 4/4 to make the shortlist of ~15):**
(a) named tech/DP practice · (b) serves mid-size companies · (c) public AI-Act activity in last
~12 months · (d) no in-house technical AI capability.

## 6. The approach sequence

Do **not** open with "do you want to partner" — it triggers the liability reflex. Lead with
usefulness; let the partnership reveal itself (same posture as the customer validation calls).

1. **Warm the target** — engage substantively with the lawyer's AI-Act content for ~2 weeks. Our technical depth vs. theirs is the opening.
2. **Non-ask first contact** — message the tech/DP partner with a *learning* frame: "I do the operational/technical side that sits under the legal advice. How are firms like yours handling the technical end when a client asks 'who actually checks our systems?' Could I get 20 minutes? Not pitching — trying to learn where the gap is."
3. **Listen for the gap, then name the demarcation** — "we handle that in-house" (deprioritise, but probe depth) vs. "honestly, that's the bit we struggle with" (the channel admitting its hole). When you hear the hole, lay out §3.
4. **Propose the lightest first collaboration** — not an exclusive. "Next time a client asks the technical question and you've nowhere to send them, try me on one. No commitment. See if it makes you look good to your client." One referral, reversible.
5. **Make referring safe & easy** — hand over the scope leaflet with the not-legal-advice statement. The easier and safer the referral, the more they make.

## 7. Partnership structures (earn left → right)

1. **Ad-hoc referral** — start here; referral fee or reciprocal.
2. **Preferred technical partner** — named go-to in their pitch materials. *Year-one goal.*
3. **White-label / sub-contract** — deliver under their brand. Higher volume, lower margin, less brand-building — useful, don't let it become the whole identity.
4. **Joint co-branded offering** — legal + technical "AI Act readiness" package. Endgame, after trust.

**Regulatory note:** in the UK, solicitors may pay/receive referral fees subject to SRA
transparency rules, and fee-sharing for *legal services* is restricted — but since we provide
**no** legal services we are largely clear. Paper the arrangement properly and let the firm's
compliance person bless the structure (another trust signal). Confirm the equivalent rules in
each target jurisdiction before signing.

## 9. Success metrics

- **30 days:** shortlist of 15 built; leaflet final; §6 sequence running on **5 firms**.
- **90 days:** ≥ 2 firms confirm the technical gap exists and are open to trying a referral.
- **6 months:** ≥ 1 live client referral serviced with an Evidence Gap Report that made the
  referring lawyer look good to their client (the trigger for referral #2).
- **North star:** 2+ mid-size firms referring repeatably — at which point the hardest problem in
  the business (trusted discovery) is solved.

## 10. Dependencies & sequencing

- **Run in parallel with the end-client validation calls** (`docs/ai-act-validation-script.md`).
  Each makes the other credible: when a lawyer asks "do companies actually want this?", we want
  evidence from the 20 conversations already in hand.

- **The legal/industry partner-hire** mentioned earlier strengthens, but is not required to
  start; prioritise a hire who brings relationships and a credential (door-opener), since we
  already have the knowledge.
