## What this is

When a company asks *"the AI Act applies to us — but who actually checks our systems?"*, that
question sits **below** the legal advice, not inside it. Legal counsel says what the law
requires. Silicon & Stone says whether the client's systems can currently **prove** they meet
it — and what to fix if they can't.

We are the operational and technical layer that turns a legal obligation into evidence.

---

## The clean split

| Your client's legal adviser owns | Silicon & Stone owns |
|---|---|
| What the law **requires** | What the client **actually has** |
| Risk classification as a **legal opinion** | Mapping the client's real AI systems to those classes |
| Interpreting the AI Act, GDPR and liability | Producing the technical evidence the law calls for |
| *"Here is your obligation"* | *"Here is whether you can prove you meet it"* |
| Sign-off, privilege, professional indemnity | The system audit, documentation and remediation plan |

> **We tell the client whether their systems can actually prove what the law requires —
> and what to fix if they can't. We never give a legal opinion.**

---

## What we are NOT

Silicon & Stone does **not** provide legal advice and is **not** a law firm. We do not
interpret statute, opine on liability, classify legal risk, or substitute for regulated legal
counsel. Any legal conclusion remains the client's adviser's to give. Where our technical
findings touch a legal question, we hand it back to counsel — we never answer it ourselves.

This boundary is deliberate. Referring a client to us should **reduce** an adviser's exposure,
not add to it: the technical audit work leaves the legal opinion — and the privilege around
it — entirely with the firm.

---

## What the client gets — the Evidence Gap Report

The core deliverable is a plain-language report that a semi-technical leadership team can act
on:

- **An inventory of the client's deployed and procured AI systems** — including the
  shadow-AI nobody signed off.
- **Each system mapped** to the documentation and controls the regulation calls for.
- **The gaps**, named specifically — what evidence exists, what is missing.
- **A prioritised remediation order** — what to fix first, and what it takes.
- **A clear marker** wherever a finding raises a legal question, flagged back to counsel.

The lawyer keeps the legal relationship. We do the operational grind underneath it — the part
that is tedious to bill and outside a legal team's technical remit.
