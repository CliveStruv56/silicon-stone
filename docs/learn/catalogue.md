# AI from Silicon to Sovereignty — Course Catalogue

Nov 3, 2026 · @Clive Struver

The catalogue is a six-level pathway of 31 courses. It runs from a free 30-minute orientation, through how AI and its infrastructure work, to governance practice and a sovereignty capstone. Silicon & Stone's own tools serve as hands-on exercises throughout. Two courses exist, one has a full outline, nine have research behind them, 14 are designed in the Beyond the Act brief, and five are new.

## Overview

**Who it serves.** Three channels share one course library:

- **Lead generation:** Level 0 and a taster lesson from each level are free, together with the free site tools.
- **Self-serve online:** Levels 1–5 as an interactive web course, bought per course or per track.
- **Paid client training:** the same modules delivered live, from a 60-minute executive briefing to a two-day workshop, leading into advisory work.

**How it grows.** The pathway follows the Beyond the Act brief: it grows the EU AI Act course *down* (foundations), *out* (the wider rulebook and standards) and *forward* (practice and security). It adds a layer the brief didn't cover: the physical infrastructure, power and supply chain behind AI.

**The sovereignty spine.** Every level asks the same question: who really controls your stack, and what are you liable for in a model you didn't build and can't audit? The question becomes a course of its own in Level 5. No competitor frames AI governance this way.

**Format of every course:**

- Slide decks first, then the interactive web version and a companion written guide.
- Two tracks in every module: **Core**, for leaders and semi-technical readers, and **Go deeper**, for engineers and specialists.
- Diagrams carry the key ideas, with UK and EU examples first.
- A practical exercise in every course, using a Silicon & Stone tool or template wherever one fits.
- All material is original Silicon & Stone content. Third-party courses and certifications (IAPP AIGP, ISACA, university programmes) shape topic coverage and structure only.

## Levels 0–1: Orientation and Foundations

This is the layer beneath compliance: the EU AI Act course governs a technology it never explains. It is free or entry-priced, and it also discharges the Article 4 AI literacy duty, in force since 2 August 2026.

### 0.1 AI in 30 minutes: from prompt to output

- **Outcome:** Learners can trace what happens between typing a prompt and getting an answer, and where the model, the data and the hardware sit.
- **Topics:** prompt → tokens → model → GPU → output; training versus inference; who runs what (you, the vendor, the cloud).
- **Source:** the Module 0 design agreed in this project.

### 0.2 AI literacy and Article 4 (F3)

- **Outcome:** Organisations can show they meet Article 4: role-mapped training, a training register and completion records.
- **Topics:** what Article 4 requires and what it doesn't; safe use at work; data you must not paste; when to escalate; shadow AI.
- **Exercise:** start a training register for one team.
- **Sources:** Beyond the Act F3; [AI Literacy Becomes the Baseline](https://app.notion.com/p/39876ad66d8c815c91c8e6ada45adbc3).

### 1.1 How AI actually works (F1)

- **Outcome:** Learners can explain in plain language what an LLM does and why it doesn't "know" things.
- **Core:** next-token prediction; training data and cut-offs; neural networks and transformers; context windows; training versus fine-tuning versus inference; what an agent adds.
- **Go deeper:** matrix maths on GPUs, memory bandwidth and HBM, why long context costs memory, open-weight versus closed models.
- **Source:** Beyond the Act F1.

### 1.2 Kinds of AI, from prediction to agents

- **Outcome:** Learners can tell a predictive model from generative AI and an agent, and match each to a business problem.
- **Topics:** narrow versus general AI; classic ML; generative models; retrieval (RAG); agents and tool use.
- **Sources:** [AI Agent Mastery](https://app.notion.com/p/3b876ad66d8c8038a316cd3e0c2bbb7b) (topics only).

### 1.3 Why AI gets it wrong (F2)

- **Outcome:** Learners can explain AI risk to colleagues and challenge a vendor's claims. Quality control of AI output is the skill workers now rate highest.
- **Topics:** hallucination as a structural property; bias and fairness through one memorable case; drift; prompt injection; over-reliance; the limits of trust.
- **Exercise:** score a vendor demo's answers for accuracy, using a short checklist.
- **Source:** Beyond the Act F2.

### 1.4 How we got here: AI winters and the revival

- **Outcome:** Learners understand why AI took off after 2010 (data, GPUs, CUDA, deep learning), which helps them judge today's hype.
- **Source:** "AI winter and the post-2010 revival" in the YouTube Content Library.

## Level 2: Infrastructure, supply chain and sovereignty

This level is the differentiator the Beyond the Act brief didn't cover: the physical stack behind AI. It draws on your Physicality of Compute method, and the Supply Chain Mapper and Scenario Modeler make it hands-on. Figures in the research notes are leads and must be checked against primary sources before they go on a slide.

### 2.1 Inside an AI server

- **Outcome:** Learners can name the parts of an AI server and rack and say what each one does.
- **Core:** GPU, HBM memory, CoWoS packaging, CPUs, networking and optics, the rack as one machine; package versus die (72 packages, 144 dies in an NVL72).
- **Go deeper:** interconnects, copper versus optical links, liquid cooling at rack level.
- **Exercise:** in the Supply Chain Mapper, find where each component in the rack is made.
- **Source:** [S&S Research Method — Physicality of Compute](https://app.notion.com/p/40576ad66d8c812096c2f27eb56147c0), series parts 1 and 4.

### 2.2 Why data centres are huge, power-hungry and expensive

- **Outcome:** Learners can explain where the power and money go, and why grid access, not chips, is often the binding constraint.
- **Topics:** power per rack, cooling, transformers and electrical steel, grid queues; the three states of capacity (announced, shipped, powered); stranded silicon and paper megawatts.
- **Exercise:** classify three real capacity announcements as announced, shipped or powered.
- **Sources:** Physicality of Compute, series parts 5 and 6; [UK Data Centre Covenant idea](https://app.notion.com/p/40976ad66d8c8147b41cea57f46d1c18) (about 73GW queued); [UK pre-Budget AI research pack](https://app.notion.com/p/40576ad66d8c81b3b927e3b6fdaecb35).

### 2.3 The compute supply chain and its choke points

- **Outcome:** Learners can map where a GPU's inputs come from and which single points of failure affect their AI plans and prices.
- **Topics:** wafers and TSMC; Japanese substrates and chemicals; HBM and the memory shortage; Chinese optics assembly; vendor financing and GPU-backed debt.
- **Exercise:** map one AI service the learner uses with the Supply Chain Mapper, then stress it in the Scenario Modeler under a Taiwan Strait or export-control scenario.
- **Sources:** Physicality of Compute, series parts 2, 3 and 7; the YouTube storyboard "Will IT hardware get cheaper in 2027?".

### 2.4 Sovereignty: what a local data centre does and doesn't give you

- **Outcome:** Learners can test a sovereignty claim by its mechanism (who controls the stack, the law and the keys), not by where the building is.
- **Topics:** data residency versus sovereignty; the CLOUD Act and extraterritorial reach; hyperscaler dependence; the UK and EU approaches compared; "sovereignty theatre".
- **Exercise:** use the Policy Stress-Test to score US–EU divergence for a cross-border deployment, then decide what a UK or EU hosting option actually changes.
- **Sources:** [European AI Sovereignty Landscape — Full Dossier](https://app.notion.com/p/39876ad66d8c81b69d73db8f40a4d363); [European AI Sovereignty vs Performance](https://app.notion.com/p/3d176ad66d8c802abeb9f2e12adaf6fb); [The Sovereign Infrastructure Window](https://app.notion.com/p/39876ad66d8c815ba149de1682c1eb07); [The Sidelining Series — Brief](https://app.notion.com/p/39876ad66d8c81db90ace58e9cd19d0d).

## Level 3: Governance, frameworks and the wider rulebook

The existing EU AI Act course stays as the Core. Around it sit the frameworks and laws the audience is caught by. Plain "what is the AI Act" courses are commoditised, so the value here is depth on standards, the GDPR and NIST splits, sectors and the wider EU digital stack, at a director-level, mid-priced point.

### 3.1 AI governance foundations

- **Outcome:** Learners understand why AI needs governance, who is accountable, and how governance runs across the lifecycle.
- **Topics:** principles (OECD, fairness, transparency, accountability); roles (provider, deployer, owner, board); risk appetite; governing development versus governing use; the lifecycle from idea to retirement.
- **Structure:** follows the domain order of the IAPP AIGP Body of Knowledge, so learners heading for AIGP are covered. All content is written fresh.

### 3.2 EU AI Act: the Core

- **Outcome:** Managers know whether and how the Act applies to them and what they must do, and when.
- **Structure:** the existing arc, modules 00–11 plus appendices, each with a deck and handout, and three facilitator agendas including a 60-minute executive one. Module 09 is split out into 3.3 and 3.6.
- **Self-serve split:** Understand (00–04), Classify and comply (05–08), Govern and plan (09–11).
- **Exercises:** classify a sample system with the Compliance Checker, then check it against the provisions corpus. The Toolkit's action plan supplies module 11's roadmap.
- **Sources:** [EU AI Act Compliance — Course](https://app.notion.com/p/c94edceb03e74a428168841e0703a88a); [Facilitator Guide](https://app.notion.com/p/32840cd5f1824320b5f75d47d6aee4a4); [Content Audit — AI Act Dates (post-Omnibus)](https://app.notion.com/p/3a376ad66d8c81459b94f9fe053d5e55).

### 3.3 NIST AI RMF as a bridge (C4)

- **Outcome:** US-exposed teams know what NIST work gives them toward the Act (roughly 60–70% control overlap, per the brief) and the EU-specific remainder it can't cover (CE marking, registration, conformity, incident reporting).
- **Structure:** the existing 60–90 minute lesson plan; Govern, Map, Measure, Manage; three worked cases (HR screening, internal assistant, agentic workflow); the Generative AI Profile.
- **Exercise:** score a deployment's US–EU divergence with the Policy Stress-Test.
- **Sources:** [Course Material Outline](https://app.notion.com/p/0eec03e4638549a8b15bd287321e6136); [Learning Hub](https://app.notion.com/p/4e9ee6e15b3144d0b0e89101b3629309); [Worked Examples](https://app.notion.com/p/3b94f501b11942ad990b3b06060bcd94).

### 3.4 ISO/IEC 42001 and the standards ecosystem (C1)

- **Outcome:** Learners know what certification does and doesn't buy (a foundation, not AI Act compliance), what it realistically costs and how long it takes.
- **Topics:** Clauses 4–10 and Annex A controls; AI impact assessment; the wider family (42005 impact assessment, 23894 risk, 42006 auditors); links to ISO 9001 and 27001.
- **Why now:** the brief found buyers screening suppliers for 42001 rose from 1% to 28% in a year. That figure is moderate evidence and needs verifying before it's used in marketing.

### 3.5 Conformity in the standards vacuum (C2)

- **Outcome:** Providers have a defensible interim approach while harmonised standards are unpublished: build against the Articles directly, use ISO drafts as stand-ins, and track CEN-CENELEC JTC 21.
- **Note:** this module is perishable and should be reviewed whenever the standards situation changes.

### 3.6 GDPR and the AI Act, properly (C3)

- **Outcome:** DPOs and privacy leads can run the GDPR side of AI governance.
- **Topics:** DPIA versus FRIA mechanics; the Article 10(5) bias-detection exception; Article 22 versus Article 86 on automated decisions; documentation with no GDPR equivalent; UK GDPR differences.
- **Source:** [EU AI Act Course 09](https://app.notion.com/p/0a39839bad1642ca99e0470bfdb9ae95), expanded.

### 3.7 The rest of the rulebook (C6)

- **Outcome:** Founders and legal leads see every instrument their AI product falls under on one map.
- **Topics:** the Cyber Resilience Act and its bridge to Article 15; the revised Product Liability Directive, which turns AI Act documentation into litigation evidence; GPSR; NIS2; the Data Act and DGA; the DSA and DMA.

### 3.8 Sector deep dives (C5)

- **Outcome:** Four short tracks on the sectors where regulation is densest: finance (DORA), health (MDR/IVDR alongside the AI Act), HR and employment (works councils, the Platform Work Directive) and the public sector.
- **Pre-reading:** the matching Silicon & Stone Sector Report.

### 3.9 The framework crosswalk

- **Outcome:** Governance leads can build one control set that satisfies the Act, NIST, 42001 and GDPR without duplicating work.
- **Format:** a capstone built around one mapping table and a single worked system that runs through all four.

## Level 4: Practice and security

This level moves from knowing to doing. Learners leave with the artefacts a compliance owner is judged on, and governance hiring is growing fastest here. Several courses ship as course-plus-Toolkit bundles.

### 4.1 Adopting AI: decisions, costs and due diligence

- **Outcome:** Executives can make and defend a build, buy or rent decision.
- **Topics:** total cost (tokens, GPUs, staff, lock-in); the pain points (data quality, integration, skills, capacity and power); when on-premises is premature; vendor ownership and compelled-access questions.
- **Exercise:** run the adoption plan through the Scenario Modeler before committing.
- **Sources:** [Consolidated Recommendations — SMB AI Operations Suite](https://app.notion.com/p/3a876ad66d8c81308a6bccc3692a1fcc); [Advisory Demand Map](https://app.notion.com/p/39876ad66d8c811f9377fe94227628f4).

### 4.2 Your AI governance operating model (I1)

- **Outcome:** Directors can charter a governance committee, name one accountable owner and map responsibilities in a RACI, choosing and adapting a model rather than copying one.
- **Deliverables:** a committee charter and RACI template, plus the Toolkit's gap assessment.

### 4.3 Building the AI system register (I2)

- **Outcome:** Learners can discover every AI system in use, including shadow AI no one procured, and record it with the fields auditors expect.
- **Exercise:** run each discovered system through the Compliance Checker, then file it in the register.
- **Sources:** [AI Systems Register (Workbook)](https://app.notion.com/p/3a376ad66d8c819ab860e4cf7af0fef4); [AI Act Compliance Toolkit](https://app.notion.com/p/3b876ad66d8c8025a391eff6d10839dd); [SME AI Act Compliance Toolkit](https://app.notion.com/p/39876ad66d8c818a9487cae5aadcad07).

### 4.4 Running a FRIA without a template (I3)

- **Outcome:** Learners can build a defensible Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment from the statutory test and combine it with an existing DPIA. The module is updated when the official template appears.

### 4.5 Vendor risk and your GPAI supplier (I4)

- **Outcome:** SMEs, as AI buyers, can use the EU model contractual clauses, apply DORA where it applies, and request a GPAI provider's Annex XII pack to build their own Annex IV documentation.
- **Exercise:** take the vendor questions the Compliance Checker generates, then score the supplier with the Toolkit's dependency scoring.
- **Source:** [AI Act preview report template](https://app.notion.com/p/3b876ad66d8c80c3b598d1a37e678fa9).

### 4.6 Incident response and human oversight (I5)

- **Outcome:** Operators can run the Article 72–73 pipeline from monitoring to reporting, against its 2-, 10- and 15-day clocks, and keep Article 14 oversight records (override logs, stop controls, escalation).
- **Format:** a tabletop exercise, not a lecture.

### 4.7 Securing AI: what Article 15 requires (S1)

- **Outcome:** Compliance and risk leads understand accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity in plain terms, what evidence a conformity assessment expects, and how this differs from hands-on red-teaming.
- **Why now:** Article 15 duties have applied since 2 August 2026, and the existing course has no security content.

### 4.8 The AI threat landscape and agentic risk

- **Outcome:** Leaders can name the main attack types on AI systems and the extra risks when agents can act.
- **Topics:** NIST's adversarial ML taxonomy (AI 100-2); the OWASP LLM Top 10 and Agentic Top 10; the UK NCSC secure AI development guidelines; model extraction; prompt injection in agent workflows.

### 4.9 Article 50, watermarking and AI-generated content

- **Outcome:** Marketing, comms and publishing teams know their labelling and disclosure duties.
- **Sources:** [EU AI Act Course 13](https://app.notion.com/p/545b463bc1d84ee4bd982973bc4691df); [EU AI Act Compliance docs](https://app.notion.com/p/3bf76ad66d8c80869150d5ae338cbce8).

### 4.10 Careers in AI assurance (elective)

- **Outcome:** Professionals can see the AI assurance roles and the skills and certifications that lead to them. This is the crossover to WaymarkPath.
- **Topics:** the certification landscape (IAPP AIGP, EXIN, ISACA AAIA and AAISM), covered as structure and comparison only.
- **Sources:** [The AI Assurance Career Track](https://app.notion.com/p/39876ad66d8c81e1b86bd7e6e2818349); [Career Sovereignty Window — Series Map](https://app.notion.com/p/39876ad66d8c81629e1dd96f3a662390).

## Level 5: Sovereignty capstone

The capstone is where Levels 2 and 3 meet: physical dependence and legal liability judged together. It is premium, close to advisory work, and something no provider in the brief's competitive scan offers.

### 5.1 The sovereignty audit: who controls your stack? (X1)

- **Outcome:** Directors map their AI vendor and embedded-model dependencies, trace training-data and jurisdictional risk, and face their liability under the Act for models they did not build, cannot audit and do not control.
- **Exercise:** a full stack audit on the learner's own organisation, combining the Supply Chain Mapper (physical dependence), the Policy Stress-Test (legal exposure) and the Toolkit's supplier scoring (contractual position).
- **Leads to:** the Strategic Assessment, which now includes the Sovereign Architecture Review.

### 5.2 European board case files (X2)

- **Outcome:** Boards and non-executive directors practise governance decisions on short simulations built from European incidents and sovereignty dilemmas, not the usual US tech-scandal set.
- **Format:** a rolling library of one-hour cases, each run through the Scenario Modeler so the board sees how the decision plays out under different futures.

## Build plan

The order follows the brief's tiers, adjusted because its two August deadlines have now passed and are live duties.

1. **Update 3.2 and 4.9.** Apply the Digital Omnibus dates (Annex III high-risk duties to 2 Dec 2027, Annex I to 2 Aug 2028) once you've confirmed they appear in the Official Journal. The Article 5 and Article 50 duties are unchanged.
2. **Build Tier 1:** 0.1, 0.2, 1.1, 1.3 and 4.7. Article 4 literacy and Article 15 security have both applied since 2 August 2026.
3. **Build the Practitioner track** (4.2–4.6) as Toolkit bundles, starting with 4.3, because every other artefact depends on the register.
4. **Turn the Physicality of Compute research into Level 2**, with the Mapper, Modeler and Stress-Test exercises.
5. **Build the electives:** 3.4 first (the strongest buy signal), then 3.6, decks for 3.3, then 3.5, 3.7, 3.8 and the 3.9 crosswalk. Then 1.2, 1.4, 3.1, 4.1, 4.8 and 4.10.
6. **Build the Level 5 capstone** last, once its inputs exist.

## Keeping it current

Much of the highest-demand material is perishable: the Omnibus timelines, the unpublished harmonised standards and FRIA template, monthly AI Office guidance and fast-moving security frameworks. Treat the catalogue as a living curriculum with a quarterly review, and flag 3.2, 3.5, 4.4, 4.6 and 4.8 for review whenever their sources change. A scheduled monitor of the Official Journal, the AI Office, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 and the EDPB could flag modules that need updating.

## Cautions

- **Third-party material shapes structure only.** The AIGP Body of Knowledge, IAPP study guides, AI Agent Mastery, AI Marketing Training and the university programmes in the brief inform topic coverage and order. All course material is written fresh.
- **Re-verify the brief's soft figures before marketing:** the ISO 42001 adoption projections, the shadow-AI percentages and the legal-hallucination statistics. The brief marks these as directional.
- **Tool status:** the live Compliance Checker is v1; v2 waits for counsel review. Check that course screenshots match whichever version is live. Sector Reports are £149 per report, including 12 months of updates, and Notion now matches the site.
